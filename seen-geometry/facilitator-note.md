# Facilitator note — Seen Geometry

Draft sample. Not official NC A&T curriculum. Do not apply.

## What not to claim

He did not teach geometry as a content expert. Lecturer courses were Critical Writing, Humanities, and Analytical Reasoning (University Studies, NC A&T, 2004–2016). As Director of Online Learning (2008–2012) he designed an online course called *Scientific Development & Social Change* — design/ops, not a math-department appointment. Do not invent labs, credentials, or “I taught geometry at A&T.” Do not introduce him as a mathematician or a software engineer. Subject facts are conservative 101. The views in Units 5 and 6 are drawn on those pages. They are teaching objects, not a school lab and not student work.

## How to run it

Keep the folder together. Open `index.html` in a desktop or phone browser, not a Drive preview. Practices are in-page JavaScript. Nothing is uploaded. Collect homework in your LMS, a shared folder, or on paper.

Eight units, in order. About four to six hours if they do the practice. Compressed coordinate geometry — not a proof course, not linear-algebra row reduction, not a graphics pipeline class. If you stretch it, use the units as weekly spines and add *your* readings. Do not add “how to build a game engine.”

Quarter turns on the page use the school rule: (x, y) → (−y, x), counterclockwise about the origin. Say that out loud if someone turns a book clockwise and thinks the page contradicted them. The book and the button can have different directions. The writeup should name which one they did.

Unit 5 draws 48 copies of one box and says so. Do not assign “count the boxes.” The job is: one mesh, many matrices. Unit 6’s sliders drive both the flat outline and the solid. If the solid does not start, grade the outline.

Do not invent a class average and paste it in.

## What to collect

| Unit | Turn-in | Skim for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Two rewritten sentences + one paragraph | A place you can name, a change you can see, what would change your mind |
| 2 | One real slide on a table | Same shift for every corner. A unit. |
| 3 | One real turn | A center named. Clockwise or counterclockwise. |
| 4 | One grow or shrink | Factor, and which point stayed (or didn’t) |
| 5 | One sentence on the field | Same mesh, different matrix. They did not inventory the boxes. |
| 6 | One blend weight in words | Morph, not a slide. 0, 1, or halfway. |
| 7 | The two orders | Endings differ: (0, 4) versus (2, 2) on the page’s square |
| 8 | Look, 250–400 words | One transform they watched. No invented matrix table. |

## Safety

Screen work and a coaster, book, or phone already on a table. No standing on a chair. No throwing. If you add a live session, you own the briefing.

## Common snags

Unit 1 written as “matrices are the language of the universe” — send them back. Unit 3 “it rotated” with no center — the center is the claim. Unit 5 counting boxes or calling them different models — one mesh. Unit 6 calling a squash a translation — the points of the shape changed. Unit 7 “order doesn’t matter” — make them press both sequences. Unit 8 inventing a tidy matrix they did not watch — one real look beats a fake table.

If someone asks whether he taught geometry at A&T: no.
