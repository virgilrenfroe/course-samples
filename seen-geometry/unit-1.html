Unit 1 · A place, then a change · ~35 minutes

Point, axis, and what a transform is

Not “over there.” A pair of numbers on axes you named, and a change a classmate could watch.

3 objectives Sort + rewrite 1 homework

What you'll do

In this sitting:

  1. Name a point as two numbers, and say which way each axis runs.
  2. Say what a transform is here: a rule that takes points to new points, which you can see.
  3. Rewrite a mood or a slogan so it names the change, a number with a unit, and what would change your mind.

Lesson

A point is a place you can name

A point, in this course, is a place named by numbers. On a flat page, two numbers are enough. (3, 2) means 3 units along the first axis and 2 along the second, after you have said where zero is and which way is positive. “The coaster is kind of near the corner” is a mood. “The coaster’s near corner is 3 centimeters right and 2 centimeters up from the table’s near-left corner” is a place. If a ruler, used the same way twice, reads 6 centimeters right, the 3 is wrong.

This is a first-year look at coordinate geometry. No calculus. You will not prove that the axes must meet at a right angle. We will use two axes that do, because that is the page you can draw. A third axis would stand up off the page. We will say so when a box in Unit 5 needs it. We will not build the algebra of that third axis.

An axis is a job, not decoration

An axis is a number line you agreed on. Zero, a positive direction, and a unit. Drop any one of those and the number is looking for a job. “I moved it 4” might be 4 centimeters, 4 inches, or 4 tiles. Those are not the same slide. First-year papers do this on schedule: a bare number, then a feeling about “space.”

The units you will actually need:

  • a length unit you can point at (centimeter, inch, or “one grid square on the page”)
  • a degree, for a quarter turn (90°) and a half turn (180°)
  • a scale factor with no unit: 2 means twice as far from the center you named; 1 means no change; 1/2 means half as far
  • a blend weight from 0 to 1, later, for a shape that mixes toward another shape

Refuse a number with no job. You do not need a conversion table.

A transform is a change you can see

A transform is a rule that sends every point to a new point. You should be able to point at the before and the after. This course uses three rules before it stacks them:

  • Translation — a slide. Every point moves by the same amount, in the same direction.
  • Rotation — a turn around a center you name. That center stays.
  • Scale — a grow or a shrink by a factor, from a center you name.

A matrix, here, is a table of numbers that can store one of those rules, or a stack of them. You will not multiply tables by hand as the assignment. You will watch the change and say which rule you saw. If a table and a picture disagree, believe the picture and check the table. “Matrices are the language of the universe” cannot be wrong, which is why it is useless here.

A claim that cannot be revised is a slogan. You need a sentence that names the change, a number with a unit (or a factor), and the look that would force a rewrite.

Teaching object · invented sentences

Four sentences a first-year might say about the same coaster on a table. They are written for this sample. They are not student records and not data from a class that ran.

  1. “That coaster feels off.”
  2. “Geometry is the hidden structure of reality.”
  3. “I moved it 4.”
  4. “I slid the coaster 4 centimeters to the right along the table’s near edge. If a ruler on that same edge reads about 2 centimeters, I drop the 4.”

Invented for this sample. Sentence 4 names the change (a slide), a unit (centimeters), and what would change your mind (the ruler).

The picture is the check

Later units put a shape on a grid and give you buttons. The button is the rule. The grid is the check. If you say “I turned it” and the marked corner only moved sideways, you slid it. The word does not win.

Hand pictures in this folder are teaching objects. Nobody filmed a class. If you slide a real coaster for homework, your centimeters are yours. Do not copy the page’s (3, 2) and call it a measurement.

Practice

Which sentence is the transform claim?

Same coaster. Pick the sentence that names a change, a unit, and what would change the speaker’s mind.

Practice

Rewrite a slogan

Slogan: “The poster got all twisty. Matrices are intense in this room.”

Rewrite it as one claim. Name the change (a slide, a turn, or a grow). Name a number with a unit, or a scale factor. Name one look that would force a revision. Avoid “feels,” “twisty,” “intense,” “language of the universe,” and any bare number.

Homework

Write it. If someone is running a section, turn it in there. 200–300 words.

Pick one object already on a table you use (a coaster, a book, a phone). Name a zero you can point at (a table corner is enough) and which way is right and which way is up. Give the object’s place as two numbers with a unit. Then name one change you could make without standing up: a slide, a turn, or leaving it. Rewrite one slogan you have heard about “space” or “matrices” so a classmate could check it. End with what would change your mind. Do not use “geometry is the language of the universe” or a number with no unit. Do not invent a class measurement.
