Unit 8 · One transform you watched · ~45 minutes

A short look

Describe one change you actually watched. A number. What would make you take the sentence back. Not a matrix table you invented to look finished.

3 objectives Spot the fake + write 1 homework

What you'll do

In this sitting:

  1. Pick one transform you can point at again: a button on an earlier unit, the five triangles, a blend weight, or the two orders.
  2. Write the rule, one number with a job, and what would change your mind.
  3. Refuse a writeup that could have been typed without looking.

Lesson

Five parts, in order

A look, here, is a short public note about one change. It is not a proof. It is not a table of matrices with three decimal places you did not watch. It is Unit 1 applied to a picture you can reopen.

(1) What you watched, and which page. (2) The rule in words: slide, turn, scale, many copies of one mesh, or a morph. Name the center if there is a turn or a scale. (3) One number with a job: units on an axis, a quarter turn, a factor, a blend weight from 0 to 1, or an ending point such as (0, 4). (4) What you actually saw, including a mis-click. (5) What would still change your mind.

If part 4 is tidier than what the page did, you wrote it before you looked. Take it off.

A teaching model — not your data

Teaching object · invented note

I watched the two squares in Unit 7. I pressed the blue Next twice. The marked corner started at (2, 0), sat at (4, 0) after the slide, and ended at (0, 4) after the quarter turn about zero. I pressed the tan Next twice. That corner went to (0, 2), then to (2, 2). I had thought a slide and a turn would land in one place. They did not.

The number I will keep is the pair of endings: (0, 4) and (2, 2). I'd drop “order matters on this square” if a reset and a second pass put both dots on the same point. I did not multiply a matrix. I did not count the copies on Unit 5. This was the page’s square, not a class I was in.

Invented for this sample as a model of tone. Not student work. Not a session at NC A&T. Virgil Renfroe did not teach geometry there.

What to refuse

Refuse “Transforms are the deep language of space. My matrix was 1.000, 0.000, 0.000.” That sentence cannot be wrong, and the table was not watched. Refuse a count of every box in the field. Refuse a blend weight you did not set. Refuse a turn with no center. Refuse a claim that the two orders in Unit 7 match, unless you show both dots on one point — which the page does not do.

You may use a book you slid on a table in Unit 2 if you would rather not use the screen again. Say the unit. Say the number. One real look. Then you write.

Homework — the look

Write it. If someone is running a section, turn it in there. 250–400 words. Look first.

Write a short look at one transform you actually watched in this folder (a slide, a turn, a scale, the five triangles or the box field, a morph weight, or the two orders). Use the five parts: what you watched and which page; the rule in words; one number with a job; what you saw, messy if messy; what would change your mind. Do not invent a tidy matrix. Do not write “geometry is the language of the universe.” Do not claim a class ran this. If a solid view did not start, say so and use a flat control on the page. If you did not look, do not submit a costume.
