Unit 7 · Do both, in both orders · ~40 minutes

Compose transforms

A slide and a turn, stacked. Slide-then-turn lands the marked corner at (0, 4). Turn-then-slide lands it at (2, 2).

3 objectives Both orders 1 homework

What you'll do

In this sitting:

  1. Run a slide and then a quarter turn, and watch the marked corner.
  2. Run the quarter turn first and then the same slide, and watch the other ending.
  3. Say, in one sentence, that the order changed the place. You do not multiply the tables by hand.

Lesson

Two rules, one after the other

A composition is one transform after another. The second rule sees the points where the first rule left them. Swap the order and the second rule has a different job, even when the two rules have the same names. “I did a slide and a turn” is not finished until you say which came first.

The square here starts with its marked corner (red or rust, one on each copy) at (2, 0). The other corners are (3, 0), (3, 1), and (2, 1). The slide is 2 units in +x. The turn is a quarter turn counterclockwise about zero: (x, y) → (−y, x). The pale square slides first. The tan square turns first. They start on the same corners, so at step 0 you mostly see one square. Step them.

Slide then turn: (2, 0) → (4, 0) → (0, 4). Turn then slide: (2, 0) → (0, 2) → (2, 2). Same two rules. Different endings.

Watch the corner, not the slogan

Press Next under the blue label once. The marked corner goes to (4, 0). That is only the slide. Press again. It goes to (0, 4). The turn took the already-slid square and swung it about zero.

Press Next under the tan label once. The marked corner goes to (0, 2). The square was still sitting at x = 2 when it turned, so it swung up, not out to x = 4. Press again. The slide adds 2 to x and the corner ends at (2, 2). It never visits (0, 4).

Matrices can store this stack. The order you apply the tables is the order of the pictures. This page will not ask you to multiply them. If a table says the two orders match, and the two dots do not, the table is wrong. The dots are the check.

A morph is not in this stack. Unit 6 changed the corner list. Here the square stays a square. Only its place and its facing change, and only because of the order.

Why the order changed the place

Homework

Write it. If someone is running a section, turn it in there. 180–280 words.

Run both sequences on this page. For each, write the marked corner at the start, after the first move, and at the end. Use the numbers the readout gives: the blue path should pass through (4, 0) and end at (0, 4); the tan path should pass through (0, 2) and end at (2, 2). If you get different numbers, say what you pressed. Then one sentence a classmate could use: what would change your mind about “order doesn’t matter.” Do not multiply matrices by hand. Do not invent a second square you did not step through.
