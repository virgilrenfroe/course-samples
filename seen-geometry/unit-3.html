Unit 3 · Name the center · ~40 minutes

Rotation — turn around an axis

A turn needs a center. Around zero, the shape swings. In place, the center stays and the corners trade jobs.

3 objectives Two centers 1 homework

What you'll do

In this sitting:

  1. Quarter-turn a shape around the origin, and again around its own center.
  2. Use the quarter-turn rule (x, y) → (−y, x), and say that this page’s buttons are counterclockwise.
  3. Name the center in a sentence. “It rotated” with no center is not finished.

Lesson

The center stays

A rotation turns a shape around a point, or around an axis through that point. In the flat page, you see the point. Every other point moves on a circle around it. The center itself does not move. If you cannot point at the center, you do not yet have a rotation. You have a gesture.

This page uses quarter turns only, so the arithmetic stays on halves and wholes. A quarter turn counterclockwise about the origin sends (x, y) to (−y, x). A clock’s hand is the other way. Clockwise about the origin would send (x, y) to (y, −x). If you turn a book clockwise on a table, say clockwise. Do not force the book to match the button. Name which one you did.

Quarter turn counterclockwise about zero: (x, y) → (−y, x). Example: (2, 0) lands at (0, 2). The origin stays at (0, 0).

Around zero is not “in place”

The shape below is a little house, not a square, so a turn does not look like the same picture with a new label. The red dot is one corner. The open dot is the house’s center, starting at (1.5, 1).

Press Around zero once. The open dot leaves (1.5, 1). The whole house swings onto another part of the grid. That is a turn about the origin — the axis through zero, sticking out of the page. Press Reset. Press In place once. The open dot is still at (1.5, 1). The red corner moves around it. Same amount of turn, different center, different picture.

Reset between the two buttons if you want a fair compare. If you stack them, you are composing transforms. That is Unit 7. The readout still tells you where the dots are.

Teaching grid, drawn for this sample. Quarter turns are counterclockwise. Not a measurement from a class.

What would change your mind

You claim a quarter turn about zero. The marked corner started at (1.0, 0.6). The rule says it lands at (−0.6, 1.0). If the red dot is somewhere else, the claim is wrong or the center was not zero. You claim a turn in place. The open dot must still read (1.5, 1) after one press from the start. If the open dot moved, the center was not the house.

A square can hide a quarter turn in place, because it looks the same after 90 degrees except for which corner is which. That is why this page uses a house. On a real book, the title is the mark. If the title still faces you, you did not turn the book, no matter what the sentence says.

Practice

Which turn is which?

From the start pose. One press each, with a reset between them.

Practice

Apply the rule once

A point at (3, 1), quarter turn counterclockwise about zero. Where does it land? Type x, y.

Homework

Write it. If someone is running a section, turn it in there. 180–280 words. Stay seated.

Turn one object already on a table (a book or a coaster) by about a quarter turn. Name the center: a corner, the middle of the cover, or a spot on the table under it. Say clockwise or counterclockwise. Say where one marked point (a title, a logo, a corner) started and where it ended, in words a classmate could check. Then write what the page’s two buttons taught you, in one sentence: around zero versus in place. What would change your mind — a photo from above, or the open dot moving when you claimed it stayed. Do not stand on a chair. If you only used this page, say which button you pressed and where the two dots ended. Do not invent a class table.
