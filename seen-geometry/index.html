Seen Geometry

A square slid three units. If the corner is still where it started, the slide did not happen.

Self-paced No login No server First-year, mixed prep

That's the job. A transform is a change you can see: a slide, a turn, a size. Later, the same shape in many places at once, and a shape that blends into another. Buttons and sliders on the page do the moves. No proofs. No row reduction.

Eight sittings. Practice in the page. Homework you write elsewhere. About four to six hours if you do the practice. Course samples

Safety — once

Screen work, plus a book or coaster already on a table if a unit asks you to slide or turn a real thing. Don't stand on a chair to film it. Don't throw anything “to see rotation.” No weapons, no “how to build.” Look from where you already sit.

What you leave able to do

  1. Name a point with two numbers on axes you stated, and say what a transform is: a change you can point at.
  2. Slide a shape and say the same shift for every point, with a unit.
  3. Turn a shape and name the center. Tell a turn around zero from a turn in place.
  4. Grow or shrink by a factor, and say whether the center moved.
  5. Look at one mesh drawn many times and say what the copies share and what the matrix changes.
  6. Watch a morph (a blend between shapes) and tell it from a slide or a turn.
  7. Do a slide and a turn in both orders and show that the endings differ.
  8. Write a short look at one transform you actually watched, and what would change your mind.
