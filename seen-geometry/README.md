# Seen Geometry — draft course sample

A playable eight-unit first-year gen-ed sample: coordinate geometry where a transform is a change you can see. Slide, turn, size, then one mesh drawn many times, then a shape that blends. Draft sample by Virgil Renfroe. **Not a live institutional course. Not official NC A&T curriculum.** Do not apply it anywhere as a catalog offering. Do not treat teaching sentences as student records or as data from a class that ran.

**Honesty lock.** He did not teach geometry as a content expert. Lecturer courses were Critical Writing, Humanities, and Analytical Reasoning. As Director of Online Learning he designed an online course called *Scientific Development & Social Change* — that is design/ops, not a math-department appointment. Do not invent “I taught geometry at A&T.” Frame: course-structure sample plus an interactive prototype. Subject facts stay at a conservative 101. No apply.

**Open the course:** keep this folder together. Double-click `index.html` in Chrome, Safari, Firefox, or Edge. Do not rely on a Google Drive (or similar) in-browser preview — the practice activities will not run. No server, no login, relative links only inside this folder. `styles.css` must sit next to the HTML files. Units 5 and 6 draw on the page. When the browser is online they load the three.js library from jsDelivr. A double-clicked file may block that download. If it does not load, the flat diagrams on those pages still carry the lesson. The course-samples site can load it.

## What the pages ask them to watch

Units 2, 3, 4, and 7 move a shape on a coordinate plane in the page. Buttons do the move. No drag-only control.

Unit 5 draws 48 copies of one box on the page: one mesh, a matrix on each copy. A few copies can drift; a button pauses that. Unit 6 draws a flat outline and a solid sphere from the same Spike and Squash sliders. No drag-only control. If the solid view does not start, the flat diagram is the lesson.

## Safety

Screen work, and a book or coaster already on a table. No standing on chairs to film a turn. No throwing objects “to see rotation.” No weapons, no “how to build.”
